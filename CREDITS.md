## What was checked and not used

- The NTSB docket (123 items total) also contains dozens of detailed,
  close-up rivet-level and interior structural-damage photographs in the
  same Structures 7F attachment (e.g. passenger-floor crossbeams, stringer
  damage) — real and public domain, but too technical/graphic for a
  general-audience case page; three representative, clearly-licensed shots
  were used instead (aircraft afloat, crane recovery, and one fuselage
  damage close-up that still reads clearly to a lay reader).
- No news-outlet photography (AP, Getty, Reuters, etc.) was used anywhere
  on this page, despite this being one of the most widely re-published
  photo events in aviation history — every incident photo here traces to
  the NTSB's own docket, not to a news wire.

Note on photo selection: the docket also contains ground-level photographs
of the burned main wreckage and house fire scene (e.g. "Photos of the
Wreckage," "Investigative Photographs," and similar generically-named
documents found via web search) — those were checked against this specific
case and rejected, either because they belong to a *different* NTSB case
entirely (mismatched docBLOB IDs returned by search engines — confirmed by
opening each PDF and reading the case number on the title page, several of
which were DCA09MA026, CEN16FA276, ERA16FA194, or DCA24LA316, not
DCA09MA027) or because, given the severity of this accident, a more
clinical/technical set of images (site location, recovered component,
recovered part) was judged more appropriate for a safety-education context
than ground-level post-fire wreckage photography. The three images used
here were all individually opened and confirmed to show "DCA09MA027" /
"N200WQ" / "Clarence Center, New York" on the source PDF page before use.
